# GPU call-graph survey of FDS time-step loops (decision D-027)

Generated by `tools/inventory/gpu_callgraph_survey.py` from FireX commit 36975d7 (all `file:line` values are for that commit; the working tree `main.f90` carries extra uncommitted lines, so the source was read from the commit). Tables: `gpu_candidate_loops.csv` (1491 loops, one row per outermost DO nest), `gpu_loop_blocker_sites.csv` (5758 rows, at most 4 sites per class per loop), `gpu_callee_routines.csv` (224 routines reachable from device-eligible loops), `gpu_array_statements.csv` (180 whole-array or section statements on mesh arrays outside DO nests), `gpu_timing_cases.csv` (119 existing CPU-timing files).

## 1. Unit of analysis and method

- A runtime routine is one of the 695 rows of `rw_matrix_routines.csv`; its stage is the `stage` column there. The main loop is main.f90:695-1213.
- A loop is an outermost `DO` statement in such a routine, found with `fortran_scope.Walker`. A `DO` whose bounds mention `LOWER_MESH_INDEX`, `UPPER_MESH_INDEX`, `NMESHES` or `N_MPI_PROCESSES` is a mesh/rank loop and is not the candidate; the nest starts at the next `DO` inside it. All 1491 outermost loops are listed; 944 are candidates because their iteration space is a mesh space.
- Iteration-space class from the loop variable and bounds text of the first (outermost) header in the nest that is a mesh space: `cell` (I/J/K or II/JJ/KK with IBAR/JBAR/KBAR-type or `*_MIN_R/_MAX_R` bounds), `face` (0-based I/J/K faces), `wall` (IW or `*WALL_CELLS`), `cfaces` (cut-face and cut-cell loops: ICF, JCF, ICC, JCC, IFACE, ...), `particle` (NLP bounds), `pencil` (every loop of `pois.f90`, the FFT/tridiagonal Poisson solver), and `other` (species, materials, ducts, devices, 1-D solid layers, tables; not candidates). Counts over all loops: other 547, cell 288, cfaces 287, pencil 205, wall 97, face 49, particle 18.
- `DO CONCURRENT` and `FORALL` do not occur: a text search of all 34 source files finds no such statement (the walker also classified 0 loops as concurrent). Whole-array or section statements on mesh arrays outside DO nests are in `gpu_array_statements.csv`: 180 statements, 0 of them containing a user-defined call.
- OpenMP evidence: 95 of the 829 device-eligible candidates have an `!$OMP DO` or `!$OMP PARALLEL DO` line directly above the header and 72 lie inside an enclosing `!$OMP PARALLEL` region (columns `omp_directive`, `omp_region_line`). The sources contain 114 `!$OMP DO`/`PARALLEL DO` lines in total.
- Output-stage loops (115 candidates) are counted and marked `host` (D-027: output is host-side); the 829 other candidates are called device-eligible below. Setup-only loops are excluded because only runtime routines are walked.

## 2. Timing basis for ranking

- FDS keeps per-subsystem CPU time in `T_USED(1:N_TIMERS)`, `N_TIMERS=16` (cons.f90:718, allocated main.f90:130), and writes `<CHID>_cpu.csv` with the header `Rank,MAIN,DIVG,MASS,VELO,PRES,WALL,DUMP,PART,RADI,FIRE,COMM,PACK,HVAC,GEOM,VEGE,CHEM,Total T_USED (s)` (main.f90:4146). Index equals header position; examples: 2 DIVG divg.f90:720, 3 MASS mass.f90:355, 4 VELO velo.f90:96, 5 PRES pres.f90:314, 6 WALL wall.f90:278, 7 DUMP dump.f90:71, 8 PART part.f90:112, 9 RADI radi.f90:3594, 10 FIRE fire.f90:1920, 11 COMM main.f90:935, 12 PACK main.f90:3381, 13 HVAC hvac.f90:760, 14 GEOM ccib.f90:1762, 15 VEGE vege.f90:155, 16 CHEM fire.f90:84. - A timer is added at the end of routines of one file, so fractions are per subsystem, not per loop. To rank loops inside a timer this survey uses a stated model, not a measurement: `score = space_weight x (statements_in_body + 0.25 x reachable_source_lines) x 3^(nest levels beyond the spatial ones)`; space_weight is 1 for a 3-D cell or face loop (times 0.1 for each missing dimension), 0.5 pencil, 0.2 wall, 0.05 cut-face and particle. `est_pct_of_total_time` = pooled timer fraction x the loop's share of the score among device-eligible candidates using the same timer. A loop's timer is that of its file (divg, mass, velo, pres, wall, part, radi, fire, ccib, vege), else that of its stage (predictor/corrector VELO, pressure PRES, radiation RADI, species_combustion FIRE, solid_phase WALL, particles PART, exchange COMM, other MAIN). GEOM, HVAC, VEGE and CHEM are zero in the pooled runs, so cut-cell loops (ccib.f90) get 0% although they are numerous; use `rank_score` for them.

## 5. What this means for K1 (restricted C++ ParallelFor) versus K2 (Fortran with OpenMP target or CUDA Fortran)

Facts only; no style is recommended here.

- 174 of 829 device-eligible loops (21%) call at least one source routine; 655 are leaf. Leaf loops with no body blocker: 160.
- The 224 reachable routines are what a device-callable form (device function under K1, `declare target` or device routine under K2) would have to cover; 31 take derived-type dummies and 37 take pointer/allocatable dummies.
- Data-layout facts under either style: 275 device-eligible loop bodies re-point a pointer with `=>` (the `POINT_TO_MESH` view style), 339 read or write allocatable/pointer components of derived types, and 83 write arrays through a subscript that is not a loop index or a function of one (`write_indirect`, `write_missing_*`); another 301 loops write with `write_derived_index` only, which includes ordinary stencil writes and needs a per-loop check.
- Lexical split, not an effort estimate: 287 loops have no body blocker or only LOCAL-class ones; 542 have a LAYOUT-class body blocker. Among the A+B routines, 276 contain a LAYOUT-class blocker and 18 only LOCAL-class ones.
- Reductions in candidate bodies: 103 loops with a scalar running update or conditional max/min pattern, 100 with an intrinsic SUM/MAXVAL/MINVAL-type call, 3 with an MPI collective.
- 95 of 829 device-eligible loops (11%) already carry an OpenMP loop directive; those lines hold the source's own `PRIVATE` lists (column `omp_directive`).

## 6. Blocker definitions and limits

Detection is lexical over `fortran_lex` logical statements (upper-cased, strings blanked) plus `fortran_scope` declaration tables. Callee findings are routine-wide (all statements of each reachable routine, not only the call path). Classes: `io` (WRITE, READ, PRINT, OPEN, CLOSE, INQUIRE, FORMAT), `alloc` (ALLOCATE, DEALLOCATE, NULLIFY, MOVE_ALLOC), `alloc_ptr_local`/`alloc_ptr_dummy` (any mention of a POINTER/ALLOCATABLE local or dummy, reads included), `dt_alloc_component` (designator reaches an ALLOCATABLE/POINTER component; ASSOCIATE names followed one level), `recursion` (RECURSIVE prefix or cycle in the static graph), `exit_out_of_nest` (EXIT of the candidate's outermost loop), `goto`, `stop`, `return`, `shutdown_call` (SHUTDOWN, SHUTDOWN_ATOM, STOP_CHECK, MPI_ABORT; SHUTDOWN writes to the error unit and sets STOP_STATUS, func.f90:120-138), `write_indirect` (assignment subscript holds a function or array lookup), `write_derived_index` (subscript holds a local variable that is not an enclosing loop index; module-level names and PARAMETERs are treated as loop-invariant), `write_missing_outer/inner_index` (non-local array whose subscripts omit a loop variable), `reduction_accum` (scalar or component updated from its own value, or conditionally on it, inside the nest), `reduction_intrinsic` (SUM, MAXVAL, MINVAL, MAXLOC, MINLOC, PRODUCT, COUNT, ANY, ALL, DOT_PRODUCT, NORM2; often on short local vectors), `reduction_mpi`/`mpi_call`/`library_call` (calls that resolve to no source routine: MPI_*, HYPRE_*, H5*, PARDISO, MKL), `save_var` (local with SAVE or an initialiser), `string_op` (CHARACTER variable or `//`), `pointer_reassign` (`=>` in the nest), `module_global_write` (scalar module variable assigned; T_USED and T_CC_USED excluded), `while_loop` (DO WHILE or unbounded DO in the nest). CYCLE of the outermost loop (`cycle_outer`) is informational and not counted as a blocker.
- LOCAL classes (alloc, alloc_ptr_dummy, alloc_ptr_local, cycle_out_of_nest, goto, io, pointer_reassign, return, save_var, shutdown_call, stop, string_op) can be edited or guarded inside the routine; LAYOUT classes (dt_alloc_component, exit_out_of_nest, library_call, module_global_write, mpi_call, recursion, reduction_accum, reduction_intrinsic, reduction_mpi, while_loop, write_derived_index, write_indirect, write_missing_inner_index, write_missing_outer_index) tie the loop to layout, index mapping, cross-iteration dependence, global effects or libraries. This split is by construct only.
- False positives: `write_derived_index` and `write_missing_*` fire on legitimate stencil or face writes and on per-thread scratch declared outside the nest; `reduction_accum` fires on counters and indices; `pointer_reassign` and `alloc_ptr_local` count harmless pointer views; callee counts are upper bounds. A scalar first assigned in the nest without reading itself is treated as private.
- False negatives: races that need dataflow, aliasing between actual arguments, writes through pointers into another mesh, calls hidden inside single-line `WHERE` bodies, module scalars changed in callees other than by `=`. Same-name or generic routines resolve to all candidates (`argres.candidates`), which can add callees. Preprocessor branches are all scanned. Labelled DO loops do not occur in the walked routines (no `DO <label>` statement was found in them).
- The stage of a routine comes from the nearest main-loop call site; a routine shared by several stages is counted once, under its primary stage. The iteration-space class is text-based, so unusual bounds land in `other` and would be missed (see `other` rows with I/J/K variables in the CSV).

## 8. Upstream merge burden (git history, window of 12 months ending at commit 36975d7)

Table `gpu_routine_churn.csv` has one row per routine containing a device-eligible candidate loop (218 routines). Generated by `tools/inventory/gpu_churn.py`.
- Method: `git log --no-merges -p -U0` over `Source/*.f90` for the window; for every touched file the post-commit blob is indexed with the same lexer (routine ranges and outermost DO nests), and each added line, and each deleted line at its hunk position, is attributed to the routine and nest that contains it at that commit. Hunk-header function context is not used. Routine identity is the id at the analysed commit, so a routine renamed inside the window is missed before the rename. A commit counts once per routine.
- Graft artifact: 1 commit in the window has no parent in this repository (its parents are cut off, so git shows every source file as added in full, 173066 added lines). It is excluded from every number below; it is a merge whose real changes cannot be recovered here, so churn from it is unknown, and a plain `git log` of any routine includes it as a whole-file addition.
- Window: 281 commits (parented, non-merge) touch Source/*.f90; 12 of them change more than 500 lines in one file and are flagged bulk. 86 commits touch at least one candidate routine (79 excluding bulk).
- (a) Commits per month touching any candidate routine: median 6.0, mean 7.2, max 16 (all commits); median 5.0, mean 6.6, max 15 (bulk commits excluded). Series, oldest month last: 4 4 10 4 6 4 6 16 5 6 12 9.
- (b) Commits per month touching the 20 candidate routines with the most commits: median 4.5, mean 5.9, max 12 (all); median 4.5, mean 5.3, max 11 (excluding bulk).
- Candidate routines with at least one change in the window: 89 of 218; with 3 or more changing commits: 30; with 10 or more: 3.
- (c) K1 view: a ported routine must be re-translated once per commit that touches it.
