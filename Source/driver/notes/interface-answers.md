## Role 3

### Flux override note, section 6

**(a) Can the per-stage flux product be a registered array without `STORE_SPECIES_FLUX` side effects?** Not today; yes with a small, additive change that does not need that flag. (read)
- The advective product `FX*UU` exists only as an inline expression in the mass update (`mass.f90` 448 and 625; the same lines in the driver's derived copy `fds_density_split.f90`). `FX/FY/FZ` and `SWORK1..4` are Fortran-side per-box scratch of the FDS mesh, not registered `MultiFab`s (`is_per_box_scratch`).
- `ADV_FX` and `DIF_FX` do exist in FDS but only when `STORE_SPECIES_FLUX` is true (allocated in `init.f90` 617; set by an output quantity in `read.f90`). The flag costs 9 more arrays of N_TOTAL_SCALARS components, extra loops, and the wrong values for your purpose: `ADV_FX` after the corrector is the average of the two stages (`mass.f90` 654), `DIF_FX` is half the sum of two stages of `-RHO_D_DZDX`.
- Proposal: add registered nodal `MultiFab`s (ng = 0, N_TOTAL_SCALARS components, same layout as the level) `ADV_FX/FY/FZ` (stage product) and `DIF_FX/FY/FZ` (stage diffusive flux) that are copied out inside the driver. ADV: in the derived density copy, right after the wall correction of `UU` and before the update, with the identical expression `FX*UU` (no change of arithmetic). DIF: `RHO_D_DZDX` is `SWORK1` (Y: `SWORK2`, Z: `SWORK3`) and is final after the species-sum and wall corrections (`divg.f90` 249, wall block 200 to 225), but the divergence `DEL_RHO_D_DEL_Z` is formed in the same routine (411): reading it at the end of the routine works for the read-out only; for the override (phase 2 before phase 3) `DIVERGENCE_PART_1` must be split or get a guarded hook (a numbered patch, 0007 or later, for the Architect, or a generated derived copy like the density split). Names of the new registry entries are additive; existing names and layouts do not change.
- Layout of these arrays follows your section 2: face `a` is the low face of cell `a`, FDS I = a - lo; the valid range is face 0..IBAR normal, 1..JBAR/1..KBAR tangential (the face values `FX` are only computed there: `GET_SCALAR_FACE_VALUE(UU,...,0,IBAR,1,JBAR,1,KBAR)`).

**(b) Do two level-0 boxes sharing a face compute bitwise the same flux today?** Yes. (run)
- `tests/check_shared_face_flux.py` on `tests/cases/dec1.fds` (1 box) and `dec2.fds` (2x2 boxes, periodic in x and z), cycle 5, predictor pass 1: `FX`, `FZ` (advective face value), `SWORK1`, `SWORK3` (`RHO_D_DZDX`, `RHO_D_DZDZ` after `DIVERGENCE_PART_1`) at all box-to-box faces and at the periodic wrap faces: 0 differing values between the two boxes that hold a face, and 0 differences from the 1-box run (cycle 1 as well). The face velocity `U`, `W` is equal in the two boxes that hold a face (0 differences); it differs from the 1-box run at round-off level in cycle 5 (the pressure solve sums in a layout-dependent order, decomposition check 2e-12), so the product `FX*UU` is bitwise equal between the two boxes but not bitwise equal to the 1-box run after the first step.
- Why it holds: the ghost cells are filled with the neighbour's interior values (`FillBoundary`) before the face value stencil, so both boxes evaluate the same operands in the same order.
- Caveat: the match is for faces between boxes of one level at the same refinement. At a coarse-fine interface the override is the only thing that makes the two sides agree, as your note says.

**(c) Do ghost faces (index -1 and IBP1) ever feed the divergence?** No. (read)
- `DEL_RHO_D_DEL_Z` (`divg.f90` 411) and the mass update (`mass.f90` 448, 625), and the heat flux divergence (`divg.f90` 394), loop over cells 1..IBAR (1..JBAR, 1..KBAR) and read faces I-1 and I: faces 0..IBAR. Face -1 does not exist in the FDS arrays (they start at 0); face IBP1 is allocated but not computed (`RHO_D_DZDX` is computed for I = 0..IBAR; `FX` for 0..IBAR). The AMReX FABs have ng = 1 for faces, so a ghost face exists as storage, but no kernel reads it for the divergence. Overrides never need to target them. Transverse index 0 and JBP1 of an x-face array are also never read.
- Section 6 item 3 (`COARSE_MESH_IF` and `UVW_SAVE`): the driver never takes the `EWC%NIC>1` branch (`wall.f90` 891) because all level-0 meshes have the same resolution, so interface walls between boxes have NIC = 1. `UVW_SAVE` at box interfaces holds the face velocity before the periodic/interface match (`fds_p_save_uvw`); a coarse-fine interface does not exist yet, so its `UVW_SAVE` rule is new work in the level generalisation [VERIFY].

### Review of `RegridInterface.H`
Overall the split of work in the header fits the driver. What I would change or need (starts only after M2a closes; the single-level driver stays the reference):
1. **Level index in `Fields` and `TimeLoop`.** `Fields` is built from one `Level0` and stores `m_l0`; `TimeLoop::Impl` has level-0 members everywhere (`each_local`, `nbox`, gather, exact sums). Change: a `Level` struct (geom, `BoxArray`, map, `ref_ratio_from_parent`, level number) that `Level0` becomes an alias of, one `Fields`/`SideData`/`BcStep` per level, and `TimeLoop` holding a vector of levels. `Fields.H` keeps its names, index maps and ghost widths (README "Layout interface"). Effort [VERIFY]: 3 to 4 days.
2. **Per-level stage functions.** `advance()` is one function in MAIN_LOOP order. It must become `stage_*(level, predictor)` functions (viscosity, mass finite differences, density, boundary step, velocity flux, divergence 1 and 2, pressure, predictor/corrector) called level by level, in the order that `FluxAccess` states: phase 1 over all levels, phase 2 overrides, phase 3 finest first. The current pass loop (CHANGE_TIME_STEP) stays global.
3. **Global dt.** `local_dt(level)` is easy (`fds_k_vpred` already returns the per-box `dt`); the MIN over boxes and ranks exists in `advance()`; extend it over levels. Same dt in both stages is what the driver does now.
4. **Split of the two kernels at the flux/divergence seam (`compute_stage_fluxes` / `apply_flux_divergence`).** Density: our derived copy is already generated and already split at the clip, so a second split at the flux product is a generator change, no FDS edit. Diffusion: `DIVERGENCE_PART_1` needs a guarded hook or a split (see (a)). The override loop must be `IF (N_OVR>0)` and write only at the listed faces; with an empty set the code path and the arithmetic are the ones of today. Test: `check_off_bitwise.sh` (OFF build), and for the ON build the final-field dump of the unchanged driver before and after the change must be byte-identical (the driver repeats bitwise), plus `run_decomp_check.sh`.
5. **`stage_flux(level, kind, dir)` as `MultiFab`:** the registered `ADV_F*`/`DIF_F*` of (a), valid for `compute_stage_fluxes` until `apply_flux_divergence`. `FluxOverride` indexing (low face of cell a; tangential J = b - lo + 1) matches `to_amrex`/`to_fds`.
6. **Coarse-fine ghost hook.** `ghost_exchange` is `FillBoundary` on one level only; the boundary step (`BcStep`) reads OMESH of boxes of the same level. A hook `fill_coarse_fine_ghosts(level, field)` called after `FillBoundary` (your `FillPatch` with the registry's ghost widths: RHO/RHOS 3, TMP/ZZ 2, others 1) at each of the five MESH_EXCHANGE positions fits. The face-centred velocity ghosts and the periodic face match need an interface rule (one velocity per interface face).
7. **Kernels on fine levels.** The FDS kernels take an FDS mesh number and read that mesh's wall cells, metrics and `OMESH`. A fine box therefore needs its own FDS mesh object (metrics RDX..RDZN, wall-cell tables for the domain boundary, no interior walls) created at `make_level`. This is the largest item (patch 0005 `POINT_TO_BOX` is still a DRAFT for that route) [VERIFY]: 5 to 8 days.
8. **Per-level `SideData` rebuild.** `SideData` is built once from FDS cells; for a new level it is solid = 0 and the face mask zero between boxes of the level; coarse-fine interface faces are no-wall faces. `make_level`/`remake_level` rebuild it from the layout alone (no FDS cell data), `clear_level` frees it. Easy; the hash function lets a test compare layouts.
9. **`LevelListener::remake_level` ordering:** old fields stay readable until Role 3 has copied; the registry must allocate the new `Fields` before the copy and free the old one after. Fine with `unique_ptr` swap.
10. Level-0 boxes keep FDS mesh order (box i is FDS mesh i+1), as the header states.

## Integration Lead (GPU spike plan)

**1. Managed vs device arena, and where a generated kernel can go.** (read, [VERIFY] where marked)
- A CUDA driver build today gives host Fortran pointers into FAB memory (`fds_alias.c` aims an array descriptor at the FAB). Device-arena FABs cannot be read by those host routines, so a device arena needs explicit staging around every host routine; the arrays each routine touches are in the stage dumps (`FDSTL_STAGE`, `FDSTL_STAGES`) and the wrappers `fds_k_*`.
- Managed arena (`amrex.the_arena_is_managed=1`): the unchanged host routines work through page migration, so it is the quickest correct first run and a way to count hand-offs; performance is not representative. A `Gpu::streamSynchronize()` is needed before any host read that follows a `ParallelFor` [VERIFY]. My recommendation follows the ruling: device arena plus staging for the measured build, managed only for debugging.
- Seams in `TimeLoop::advance()`: the stage calls (`fds_k_visc`, `fds_p_mfd`, `density`, `fds_k_vflux`, `fds_k_div1/2`, `fds_k_vpred/vcorr`, boundary steps). (i) Best seam: a loop inside the **derived** density copy (`fds_density_split.f90`, generated by `tools/gen_density_split.py`): replacing the ten WP0 kernels there needs no FDS patch and no extra hand-off beyond the routine's own. (ii) Whole routines replaced by a derived copy (same method) once every loop of the routine is covered by generated kernels. (iii) A hook patch like 0003/0004 (`IF (EXTERNAL_KERNEL) CALL ...` inside the FDS routine, guarded by `WITH_AMREX`) is the right tool for one loop inside a big routine (for example one loop of `COMPUTE_VISCOSITY`), at the price of one hand-off per hook. I would use (i) and (ii) first and (iii) only where a routine has one or two non-covered loops.

**3. Periodic 3-D case of 128^3 or more with a CPU baseline.** Done, run. (run)

**4. Host routines left in a fully periodic step; could a device-only path be bitwise to the BCCHAIN test?** (read)
- Per step: `COMPUTE_VISCOSITY` (2x), `MASS_FINITE_DIFFERENCES` (2x), density (+ clip), `FillBoundary` (5 positions), `fill_omesh` (host broadcast, after the match and at each exchange 5), `VISCOSITY_BC` (2x, then the domain edge/corner copies of `MU`, `KRES`), `WALL_BC` (predictor after the velocity flux, corrector, and the one in the boundary chain), `MATCH_VELOCITY` (driver: `match_periodic_faces`, FDS's own is skipped), `VELOCITY_BC` (2x), `VELOCITY_FLUX` (2x), `DIVERGENCE_PART_1/2` (2x), the pressure call, `VELOCITY_PREDICTOR/CORRECTOR` with the stability check, the zone sums and `ExactSum`. All run on host data today; periodic faces are still `INTERPOLATED` wall cells in the FDS data model.
- A device-only periodic path replaces: the ghost fill (`FillBoundary` runs on device), the face match (one expression `0.5*(a + ((b*dA1)*dA2)/(dA1*dA2))`, reproducible with `--fmad=false`), the `MU`/`KRES` edge copies (pure copies), `fill_omesh` (not needed), and the host wall tables that kernels read. The remaining arithmetic is in `VELOCITY_BC` (edge `OMEGA`/`TAU` from `MU` averages and velocity differences) and `VISCOSITY_BC`/`WALL_BC` for interpolated walls. If those formulas are generated with the same operation order and no FMA, bitwise equality with the BCCHAIN tags is plausible; the chain is copies plus a few edge formulas, not a solve, so there is no order-dependent reduction [VERIFY: the edge formulas are the risk, and the kernel check tags would be the test].

**5. Who converts the RHS copy, `CommonLayer` loops and `ExactSum`; separate-prefix AMReX CUDA build.** (view)
- RHS copy (call site in `TimeLoop::solve_poisson`, Fortran to host buffer to `MultiFab`): Role 1, as a `ParallelFor` on the device arena once the divergence arrays are device-resident (WP5 call-site part). `CommonLayer` loops (`apply_operator`, mean removal, residual, pins) and the FFT/MLMG call: Role 2 (they own `pressure_backend`); I will not edit them. `ExactSum` (driver file): Role 1. Suggestion: the fixed-point scheme (integers scaled by a global maximum, order-independent integer sums) is device friendly with 64-bit integer atomics, which would keep the sums bitwise equal between CPU and GPU, better than the fixed-order tree of ruling (c) [VERIFY: 128-bit accumulation needs two 64-bit limbs with carry handling].
- A separate-prefix AMReX CUDA build with MPI and FFT is acceptable to me for the driver: the driver finds AMReX through `find_package`, so the CPU install stays untouched and the same source commit keeps results comparable. Conditions: the same MPI for AMReX and the FDS Fortran (the plan lists Intel MPI for the CUDA install; the FDS side and the Fortran `mpi` module must be built with that MPI, a separate driver build tree); `AMReX_FFT=ON` (cuFFT); host compile flags without FMA contraction (`-ffp-contract=off`) for the FDS and driver translation units, as the plan's NFR-051 line says; OpenMP is not needed by the driver (threads = 1 in all checks), so OpenMP off is fine [VERIFY: whether the FDS objects are built with `-fopenmp` while AMReX is not]. The exit code 139 at teardown seen in the harness should be understood before the driver is linked (RDC and the Fortran runtime are suspects).
